### 1. What Stitch IS and IS NOT good at in 2026

**Where Stitch shines:**
*   **Tailwind UI Polish:** It is exceptional at taking a functional, boring Tailwind component and making it look like a premium enterprise site. It understands spacing, typography hierarchy, and subtle micro-interactions (hover states, focus rings) perfectly.
*   **Strict Token Adherence:** If you feed it your exact `tailwind.config.ts`, it will not hallucinate colors. It will stick to your Navy/Steel/Bluegray palette.
*   **SVG Generation:** It is great at generating abstract, brand-aligned SVG background patterns (e.g., subtle geometric architectural lines for your Hero background).

**Where Stitch falls short (DO NOT use it for this):**
*   **B2B Industrial Product Photography:** Stitch uses Google’s Imagen models under the hood. While great for consumer marketing, **it will fail at generating physically accurate fiber-cement subfloors on steel joists.** It will hallucinate incorrect joist spacing, unsafe construction practices, or wrong material textures. 
*   **Complex Server Components:** Stitch generates *client-side* React UI. It does not understand Next.js 15 App Router data fetching or your `data/products.ts` contract natively.

**The Verdict:** Use Stitch to redesign the *components* (HTML/Tailwind). **Do not use Stitch to generate the 9 product photos.** Use Midjourney v6/v7 for the photography, or buy premium stock photos of generic construction sites.

### 4. The Integration Handoff Workflow

Because you use Claude Code in VS Code, the handoff from Stitch is highly efficient. Do not try to copy-paste Stitch code directly over your files, as it will break your Next.js Server Component contracts.

**The Workflow:**
1. Generate the visual design in Stitch until you like it.
2. Click "Export Code" (React/Tailwind) in Stitch. Copy it to your clipboard.
3. Open VS Code. Open your existing component (e.g., `components/ProductCard.tsx`).
4. Open Claude Code and use this exact prompt:
   > "I have generated a new visual design for `ProductCard.tsx` using Google Stitch. Here is the Stitch code: [PASTE STITCH CODE]. 
   > Please update my existing `components/ProductCard.tsx` to use this new Tailwind markup and styling. 
   > **CRITICAL:** You must preserve my existing TypeScript interfaces, the `data/products.ts` prop mapping, and keep it as a React Server Component. Do not add any client-side hooks (`useState`) that Stitch might have included for prototyping."

### 6. Risk Warnings Specific to JARA

1.  **The PLYCEM Trademark Trap (SB-5/SB-7):** AI image generators *love* to invent logos and slap them on products. If you use Midjourney, you must rigorously check that no fake "PLYCEM" or generic logos appear on the panels. If they do, you violate your factory contract.
2.  **The Warehouse Trap (ADR-049):** You have no US warehouse. If you prompt an AI for "inventory" or "pallets," it will generate a massive warehouse background. If a US buyer sees this, they will assume you hold domestic stock, leading to broken trust when you tell them it's direct-factory shipping. **Only generate images of products *installed* on job sites, or isolated on studio backgrounds.**
3.  **The Cert-Gap Warning Erasure:** Stitch's AI will look at your `CertGapWarning` component (the amber warning for lap-siding/corrugated-roof-tile) and think, "This is ugly, let me make it a subtle, pretty gray." **Do not let it.** That warning is a legal/compliance necessity. If Stitch softens it, manually revert the background to an aggressive amber/yellow.
4.  **Color Bleed:** Stitch might try to introduce a "primary blue" (like Google's blue) for buttons. Your brand guide explicitly forbids bright royal blue. Always double-check that buttons remain `bg-navy` or `bg-steel`.
